BIG IDEA

The behavior of gases can be explained through the Kinetic Molecular Theory (KMT), which relates molecular motion to macroscopic properties like pressure and temperature. However, "real" gases deviate from ideal behavior under high pressure and low temperature due to molecular volume and intermolecular forces (IMFs).

KEY TERMS

  • Partial Pressure ($P_i$): The pressure exerted by an individual gas in a mixture.
  • Mole Fraction ($\chi$): The ratio of the moles of a specific component to the total moles in a mixture. It has no units.
  • Kinetic Molecular Theory (KMT): A model explaining gas behavior based on random motion, negligible molecular volume, and lack of attractive forces.
  • Effusion: Gas escaping through a tiny hole into a vacuum.
  • Diffusion: The spread of one substance through the space of another.
  • Intermolecular Forces (IMF): Attractive forces between molecules (as opposed to intramolecular bonds within a molecule).

STEPS/PROCESS

Calculating Partial Pressure via Mole Fraction

1. Find Moles of each component:

Convert grams to moles using molar mass if necessary.

2. Calculate Total Moles ($n_{total}$):

$$n_{total} = n_1 + n_2 + ... + n_n$$

3. Find Mole Fraction ($\chi_i$):

$$\chi_i = \frac{n_i}{n_{total}}$$

4. Apply to Total Pressure ($P_{total}$):

$$P_i = \chi_i \times P_{total}$$

RULES & IDENTITIES

Dalton's Law & Mole Fractions

  • $P_{total} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3...$
  • The sum of all mole fractions in a mixture must equal 1 (analogous to 100%).
  • Partial pressure is directly proportional to mole fraction: If a gas makes up 30% of the moles, it exerts 30% of the total pressure.

Kinetic Molecular Theory (KMT) Tenets

  1. Gases consist of molecules in continuous random motion.
  2. The volume of gas molecules is negligible compared to the container (mostly empty space).
  3. Attractive/repulsive forces between gas molecules are negligible.
  4. Average kinetic energy ($KE$) is proportional to absolute temperature (Kelvin).
Laws vs. Theories: A Law (like Boyle's Law) tells you what happens (e.g., $P \uparrow, V \downarrow$). A Theory (KMT) explains why it happens (e.g., particles hitting walls more frequently).

Van der Waals Equation (Real Gases)

To correct for real gas behavior (particle volume and attractions), we use:

$$\left( P + \frac{an^2}{V^2} \right)(V - nb) = nRT$$

  • Term $a$: Accounts for attractive forces (reduces pressure).
  • Term $b$: Accounts for the volume occupied by gas particles (reduces available volume).
  • Gases behave most ideally at High Temperature and Low Pressure.

EXAMPLE 1: Partial Pressure of Argon

Problem: Calculate the partial pressure of Argon in a container with 2.3 moles of Ar and 40.0g of Neon. Total pressure is 841 torr.

1. Find Moles of Neon:
$40.0g \text{ Ne} \times \frac{1 \text{ mole}}{20.18g} = 1.982 \text{ moles Ne}$

2. Total Moles:
$2.3 \text{ (Ar)} + 1.982 \text{ (Ne)} = 4.282 \text{ moles total}$

3. Mole Fraction of Ar ($\chi_{Ar}$):
$\chi_{Ar} = \frac{2.3}{4.282} = 0.537$ (Argon is 53.7% of the mixture)

4. Partial Pressure of Ar ($P_{Ar}$):
$P_{Ar} = 0.537 \times 841 \text{ torr} = 452 \text{ torr}$

EXAMPLE 2: Velocity and Mass

Root Mean Square Velocity Equation: $$u_{rms} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$$

Logic: If mass ($M$) increases, velocity decreases. If Temperature ($T$) increases, velocity increases.

Question: Which has the greatest average speed? (A) $He$ at 500K, (B) $He$ at 300K, (C) $N_2$ at 500K.

Answer: (A). Helium is the lightest ($4g/mol$ vs $28g/mol$) and it is at the highest temperature.

INTERMOLECULAR FORCES (IMFs)

Force Type Description Strength
London Dispersion (LDF) Instantaneous/induced dipoles. Occurs in all molecules. The only force for non-polar molecules. Increases with size/surface area. Weakest
Dipole-Dipole Permanent dipoles in polar molecules. Opposite partial charges attract. Intermediate
Hydrogen Bonding Special strong dipole-dipole. Requires H bonded to N, O, or F interacting with N, O, or F on another molecule. Strongest
Ion-Dipole Interaction between an ion (like $Na^+$) and a polar molecule (like $H_2O$). Explains how salt dissolves. Very Strong
IMFs and Phase: At room temperature, weak IMFs lead to gases. Moderate IMFs lead to liquids. Strong IMFs lead to solids. Higher IMFs always result in higher Boiling Points and Melting Points because more energy is required to pull the molecules apart.
