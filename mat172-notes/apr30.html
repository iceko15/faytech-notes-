While it is comprehensive, it focuses on general, high-level questions rather than the most difficult niche problems. RULES & IDENTITIES TO MEMORIZE

Important: If it's not on the provided 2-page formula sheet, you must know it. This includes variable definitions and basic notations.
  • Reciprocal Identities: $\tan \theta = \frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta}$ and $\cot \theta = \frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta}$.
  • Pythagorean Identities: Recognize all versions, including rearranged forms (e.g., $\sin^2 \theta = 1 - \cos^2 \theta$).
  • Dot Product: The dot product formula is not on the sheet. It results in a scalar (no $i$ or $j$).
  • Notation Differences:
    • Real Numbers: $(x, y)$ or $(r, \theta)$
    • Imaginary Numbers: $a + bi$ (Modulus is hypotenuse)
    • Vectors: $ai + bj$ (Magnitude is hypotenuse)

STEPS/PROCESS: VECTOR RESULTANTS

  1. Sketch the Vectors: Adjust navigational bearings to standard rotation angles (relative to positive x-axis).
  2. Decompose: Write each vector in component form: $V = \langle |V|\cos\theta, |V|\sin\theta \rangle$.
  3. Sum Components: Add the $i$ components together and the $j$ components together to get the Resultant Vector ($R$).
  4. Calculate Magnitude: Use $|R| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.
  5. Calculate Direction: Use $\theta = \tan^{-1}(\frac{b}{a})$. Treat the calculator output as a reference angle and adjust based on the quadrant of $R$.

EXAMPLE: CONIC MATCHING (SHORTCUTS)

  • Missing a square term: Parabola.
  • Both terms squared with a MINUS: Hyperbola.
  • Both terms squared with a PLUS:
    • Coefficients same: Circle.
    • Coefficients different: Ellipse.

PROFESSOR TIPS FOR THE FINAL

  • Calculator Mode: Whenever you touch Sin/Cos/Tan, check if you are in Degrees or Radians.
  • Inverse Trig: The calculator only gives one answer based on function restrictions (e.g., Quadrant 1 or 4 for Sine). You must interpret if the problem requires a second solution in a different quadrant.
  • Order: The questions on the final are "quasi-in-order" but somewhat scrambled. Don't assume Question 7 must be from Section 4.7.
  • Brownie Points: If you do the "Yellow Handout" review on paper and show all your work, it can help with creative rounding (e.g., bumping an 89.3 to an 90). Effort matters!
