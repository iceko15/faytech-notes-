BIG IDEA

Mastering the identification, conversion, and graphing of Conic Sections (Parabolas, Ellipses, Hyperbolas) in both rectangular and polar forms, while preparing for a comprehensive final exam that integrates trigonometry, vectors, and complex numbers.

KEY TERMS

  • Standard Form: The specific arrangement of a conic equation that allows for the immediate identification of vertices, foci, and orientation.
  • General Form: An expanded quadratic equation $Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$. Converting this to standard form requires completing the square.
  • Eccentricity ($e$): A measure of how "un-circular" a conic is.
    • $e = 1$: Parabola
    • $0 < e < 1$: Ellipse
    • $e > 1$: Hyperbola
  • The Pole: In polar conics, one focus is always located at the pole (the origin $(0,0)$).
  • Directrix: A fixed line used to define the conic section; its distance from the focus is denoted as $p$ or $k$.

POLAR CONICS & CALCULATOR STRATEGY

The standard template for polar conics is:

$$r = \frac{ep}{1 \pm e \cos \theta} \quad \text{or} \quad r = \frac{ep}{1 \pm e \sin \theta}$$

Analysis of $r = \frac{8}{2 - 4 \cos \theta}$:

  1. Normalize: Divide everything by 2 to get a leading 1 in the denominator. $$r = \frac{4}{1 - 2 \cos \theta}$$
  2. Identify $e$: Here $e = 2$. Since $e > 1$, it is a Hyperbola.
  3. Orientation: A negative cosine indicates the hyperbola opens horizontally, with the right-hand wing opening toward the right.
  4. Calculator Table Set: Set $\Delta Tbl$ to $\pi/12$ or $\pi/2$. $\pi/2$ is usually sufficient to find the 4 quadrantal "landmark" points (vertices and intercepts).

PROCEDURAL NOTES: SOLVING TRIANGLES

When solving triangles (Law of Sines/Cosines):

  • SSA (Side-Side-Angle): The "Ambiguous Case." Always check for a second triangle.
  • Common Error: Forgetting to square root the result of the Law of Cosines when solving for a side. If $c^2 = 400$, then $c = 20$. Skipping this throws off all subsequent angle calculations.
  • Vector Notation: Don't lose the $i$'s and $j$'s during calculation; confusing vectors with imaginary numbers or real numbers will lead to notation errors.
