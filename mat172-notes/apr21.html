Polar Conic Sections & Eccentricity

BIG IDEA

In rectangular coordinates, conics are defined by vertices, axes, and focal distance. In Polar Form, we fixate our attention on the Focus and the Directrix. All polar conic sections are situated so that one of their foci is located at the Pole (the intersection of the horizontal and vertical axes, formerly $(0,0)$ in rectangular space).

Warning on "e": In this context, $e$ stands for Eccentricity, which is a ratio of distances. This is NOT Euler's number ($e \approx 2.718$). Do not mash the $e$ button on your calculator for these problems!

KEY TERMS

  • The Pole: The origin of the polar coordinate system. One focus of the conic must sit here.
  • Eccentricity ($e$): The ratio of the distance from the focus to a point on the graph, over the distance from that point to the directrix.
    • $e = 1$: Parabola (Distances are equal).
    • $e < 1$: Ellipse (Numerator is shorter than denominator).
    • $e > 1$: Hyperbola (Numerator is longer than denominator).
  • Directrix ($k$ or $p$): A fixed line. The distance from the focus (at the pole) to the directrix is denoted as $k$.
    • $x = k$ (Vertical line to the right).
    • $x = -k$ (Vertical line to the left).
    • $y = k$ (Horizontal line above).
    • $y = -k$ (Horizontal line below).

STEPS/PROCESS

Step 1: Convert to Standard Form

Your equation must look like one of the four standard polar templates. The first term in the denominator must be a 1. If it is a 5 or a 2, you must divide every term in the numerator and denominator by that number to "force" the 1 into place.

Step 2: Identify $e$ and $k$

Once in standard form, $e$ is the coefficient in front of the sine or cosine. The numerator is equal to $k \cdot e$. Use this to solve for $k$.

Step 3: Determine Orientation

Denominator Format Orientation / Directrix
$1 + e \cos \theta$ Opens left; Directrix is vertical at $x = k$.
$1 - e \cos \theta$ Opens right; Directrix is vertical at $x = -k$.
$1 + e \sin \theta$ Opens down; Directrix is horizontal at $y = k$.
$1 - e \sin \theta$ Opens up; Directrix is horizontal at $y = -k$.

RULES/IDENTITIES

  • Standard Form: $r = \frac{ke}{1 \pm e \cos \theta}$ or $r = \frac{ke}{1 \pm e \sin \theta}$
  • Rectangular Form: $x = \text{vertical line}$, $y = \text{horizontal line}$. MyMathLab usually wants directrix equations in rectangular form.
  • Calculator Setup: Use Polar Mode and Radian Mode. When keying in, encapsulate the entire denominator in parentheses: 6 / (2 - 2 sin(theta)).

EXAMPLE 1: Parabola

Equation: $r = \frac{6}{2 - 2 \sin \theta}$

1. Divide by 2 to get the leading 1 in the denominator:

$$r = \frac{3}{1 - 1 \sin \theta}$$

2. Identify $e$ and $k$:

  • $e = 1$ (This is a Parabola).
  • Numerator: $ke = 3 \Rightarrow k(1) = 3 \Rightarrow k = 3$.

3. Orientation: The "minus sine" indicates the graph opens up. The directrix is below the pole at $y = -3$.

4. Vertex: Use the table feature at $\theta = \frac{3\pi}{2}$. $r = 1.5$. Vertex: $(1.5, \frac{3\pi}{2})$.

EXAMPLE 2: Ellipse

Equation: $r = \frac{12}{5 + 3 \cos \theta}$

1. Divide by 5 for Standard Form:

$$r = \frac{12/5}{1 + \frac{3}{5} \cos \theta}$$

2. Identify $e$ and $k$:

  • $e = \frac{3}{5}$ ($e < 1$, so this is an Ellipse).
  • Numerator: $ke = \frac{12}{5} \Rightarrow k(\frac{3}{5}) = \frac{12}{5} \Rightarrow k = 4$.

3. Orientation: "Plus cosine" means it opens left. Directrix is at $x = 4$.

4. Vertices: Check quadrantal angles ($\theta = 0, \pi$).

  • At $\theta = 0, r = 1.5$. Vertex: $(1.5, 0)$.
  • At $\theta = \pi, r = 6$. Vertex: $(6, \pi)$.

EXAMPLE 3: Hyperbola

Equation: $r = \frac{8}{2 - 4 \cos \theta}$

1. Divide by 2:

$$r = \frac{4}{1 - 2 \cos \theta}$$

2. Identify $e$ and $k$:

  • $e = 2$ ($e > 1$, so this is a Hyperbola).
  • Numerator: $ke = 4 \Rightarrow k(2) = 4 \Rightarrow k = 2$.

3. Orientation: "Minus cosine" opens right. Directrix is at $x = -2$.

4. Calculator Note: Hyperbolas often show "happy glitches" (lines connecting the branches). These are essentially the asymptotes.

SUMMARY / EXAM TIPS

  • MyMathLab Fractions: The system hates decimals. If you see $1.4545...$ in your table, plug the angle back into the original equation to find the exact fraction (e.g., $16/11$).
  • Naming Points: Do not use coterminal or negative $r$ names if you can avoid it. MyMathLab prefers the primary positive rotation found in your table.
  • Final Exam Prep: You must know the unit circle, linear/angular speed, and SOH-CAH-TOA. Polar conics will be on there, but you won't have the orientation pictures—just the verbal descriptions.
